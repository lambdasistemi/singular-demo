The steps above show what Demo 1 does. Some limits of its design cannot be seen in a command, or only as a refusal. They are stated here as they hold in this release (v0.14.0). Each one says how it is known: shown in the steps, tried on a private chain like this one without being part of the steps, or taken from the project's documentation.

**A booking reserves nothing.** Tried on a private chain. Two people can book the same key, and both bookings succeed. Only the fold decides: it takes one of the two requests and leaves the other out, and its receipt names the reason (`key-in-batch`: an earlier included request names the same key). In the run that was tried, the fold took the request booked first. The request left out stays pending with its deposit locked, until its owner takes it back with a reclaim while the retract window is open.

**A stuck request is cleared with all the others or not at all.** Tried on a private chain. A reject takes every pending request. While any one of them is still inside its processing window or its retract window, the program refuses to build the reject, even when another request is long past both windows and could be rejected alone. A stale request can therefore wait for a moment when nothing else is inside a window. Two things limit the damage: the owner can take the request back with a reclaim while the retract window is open, and a stale request does not stop the registry. A fold of another key went through while one was pending, and the fold's receipt listed the stale request as left out. The reject itself is a known limit of this release, tracked in [issue 487](https://github.com/lambdasistemi/singular/issues/487).

**Folding is open to everyone and required of no one.** Shown in the steps. Bob folds Alice's request and Alice folds Bob's, and a request nobody folds, like `late`, stays pending. The design does not promise that a request is ever folded. It gives its owner the two ways out seen above, the reclaim and the reject.

**A fold has a capacity.** Shown in the fold's receipt, which states the capacity as nine requests and calls it "an estimate, not a measurement: about nine requests fit in a fold under preprod's execution budget of 14 million memory units, bound by trie-proof verification". The steps fold one request at a time and never come near it.

**A booking that never lands is not recovered by waiting.** From the documentation. A fold carries a deadline; a booking does not. A booking that did not reach the chain, or was rolled back and not included again, stays unresolved however far the chain moves on, and every later write stops on it. The documentation calls this a [known limit of the commands](https://lambdasistemi.github.io/singular/docs/cli-recovery/#when-a-transaction-can-never-land) The steps above did not meet it.

**A key does not come back.** Shown in the steps. A key is added once and can be ended for good; nothing returns it to unknown, and `alice` stays terminal. Reusable names are not part of this release; the contract admits only [these two changes](https://github.com/lambdasistemi/singular/blob/v0.14.0/onchain/validators/state.ak).

**The application protects the payload and the deposit, nothing more.** From the documentation. `open-datum` is a demonstration of the registry's mechanics, not a way to guard names or secrets. The project [says the same](https://lambdasistemi.github.io/singular/docs/demo1-devnet/#what-this-does-not-claim).

**This is one play on a private chain.** The windows here are three minutes and one minute so that the waits stay short; the defaults are ten minutes and five. The programs read the chain through a provider (here the devnet's own, which answers like a Koios): its answers are taken as claims, and the several reads behind one command need not come from one chain state. Most of this journey has also been played on the public preprod network. What has run where, and what has not, is [listed in the documentation](https://lambdasistemi.github.io/singular/docs/demo1-preprod/).
